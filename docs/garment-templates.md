# Garment templates — pattern-piece UVs and "texture from cut"

A worn piece in mode `template` (`outfit_piece.mesh_mode`) is not generated by an image-to-mesh backend: Blender
cuts it from sewing-pattern parameters, drapes it on the character's base body and textures it
(`app/core/garment_template.py`, Blender scripts `app/blender/scripts/garment_template_build.py` /
`garment_template_drape.py`). This page covers the part added by the plan
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-20-template-cut-uv-texture.md`: the UV layout of a top as real sewing-pattern pieces
and the second texture path, "texture from cut". The checked facts below are guarded by
`scripts/smoke_docs_garment_templates.py`.

Template version: **`TEMPLATE_VERSION` 10** (the bump that introduced the pattern UV; every drape of an older version
is out of date and is re-draped by the next Build).

## 1. Pattern-piece UV islands (step 1: tops)

### Which pieces get them — `uv_mode`

`garment_template.build_args` passes the build arg `uv_mode`: `"pattern"` for `top`, `briefs`, `bra`, `gloves`,
`tights`, `trousers`, `skirt` (`PATTERN_KINDS`; the close-fitting kinds since step 3a, section 1a; a skirt and a
strapless top since step 3b, section 1b), `"grid"` for every other kind (jacket, dress). `"grid"` is staging, not a
compat path: the later phases of plan group B move the rest to the pattern UV and remove it.

### Parts come from the construction

`build_bodice` labels every face with its pattern part at the moment it creates it (torso/yoke faces by
construction column, sleeves by side, the neckband by ring) and marks the underarm and band cut edges by construction
index. `run()` writes them as bmesh int layers `uv_part` (per face) and `uv_seam` (per edge, 0/1), so they survive
the face filter, `dissolve_degenerate`, `clear_of_body` and `split_slit`; then `pattern_uv_of` hands them to the
pure-numpy module `app/blender/scripts/uv_pattern.py` (no bpy, no scipy; `scripts/smoke_uv_pattern.py` checks it
without Blender). Nothing is ever relabelled: a part that falls into several connected components becomes `name`,
`name_2`, … (an open front would give `front` and `front_2`).

Part rule for a torso or yoke face of column `c` (the face between columns `c` and `c+1`, `N` columns, column 0 =
front centre): **front** when `c < N/4` or `c >= 3N/4`, else **back**. The side seam is the column `N/4` / `3N/4`
below the armhole, the shoulder seam the yoke meridian of that column (from the top of the armhole to the side of the
neck). The innermost yoke ring is the **band** whenever the piece has a neck ring (not `straps`, not off-shoulder).

### Flattening and orientation

- A **panel** is flattened from its BUILT rest geometry: an LSCM start, then ARAP (cotangent weights, a
  hand-written conjugate gradient). Measured on three real tops: area ratio 0.995–0.999, 95th-percentile stretch
  ≤ 1.14.
- A **strip** (the band) is not flattened: u = arc length along the band's mid line, v = across, the neck edge up,
  u running from the back centre through the wearer's right, the front and the left back to the back centre (the
  order seen from outside). A flattened neck ring would be a C-shaped annulus, not the straight strip a pattern has.
- **Orientation** (`uv_pattern.orient`): every island is mirrored so it is seen from OUTSIDE (area-weighted sign of
  triangle normal · outward against the sign of the UV area — never the winding) and rotated so its grain points
  up: front/back `up = +Z` around the vertical axis through the torso centre; sleeves `up` = hand → shoulder around
  the arm line. The front therefore shows the character's left (+X) at image right, the back shows −X at image right.
- **Sleeve cap**: every sleeve island records `cap_height_cm` = v(shoulder point of the armhole loop) − mean
  v(the two underarm seam ends), signed (negative = inverted). Measured: +10.6 to +14.4 cm on the tops of the plan's
  Task 4 — a positive bell. The armhole geometry is unchanged by this plan; a different cap shape is a geometry change.

### One density, one atlas

`uv_pattern.pack` puts all islands at **one px/cm** into a square atlas of **`PATTERN_ATLAS_PX` = 2048 px** with
**`PATTERN_MARGIN_CM` = 1.5 cm** around every island, in rows (`ROW_ORDER`: front/back, the sleeves, the strips),
px/cm = floor to 0.01 of 2048 / the layout extent in cm (measured 13.8–18.4 px/cm). The projection bake writes the same
2048 px atlas for a pattern piece (the grid UV keeps 1024). The assembly grafts the PNG unchanged into both figure
tiers — no downsizing.

### `islands.json`

The drape keeps its islands beside the GLB as `templates/{item}_{tsig}.islands.json` (`garment_template.islands_path`,
under `characters/<name>/garments/`); the drape record's `islands` = `{file, sha256, px_per_cm}`. Payload
(`uv_pattern.islands_payload`): `version`, `atlas_px`, `px_per_cm`, `margin_cm`, and per island `name`, `part`,
`bbox_px`, `loops_px`, `verts_px`, `tris`, `area_cm2`, `distortion`, `cap_height_cm` (sleeves), plus `seams` — matched
point pairs on both sides of every seam (`seam_samples`, one per cm). Pixels are IMAGE-frame (x right, y down; Blender
UV = x/size, 1 − y/size). The payload holds no timing or run data, so its sha is a pure function of the geometry.
Blender writes no island PNG: label rasters and masks are rendered in the core from this file
(`garment_cut_texture.rasterise_islands` / `island_mask`).

### The cut without the drape (cut-first, 2026-11-21)

The pattern UVs are assigned to the cut panels BEFORE the cloth run (`build_bodice` → `clear_of_body` → `pattern_uv_of`
in `garment_template_build.run`); the drape only moves vertices. So the islands depend on the cut alone, and
`app/core/garment_template_cut.py` makes them without a drape: the same builder with the build arg `cut_only` stops
right after `pattern_uv_of` (no drape, no pose shapes, no bake, no export). CPU, one Blender slot, **0 hub jobs**, and
never the piece's hub-job claim (`piece_jobs`) — it may run beside a drape. Measured on a copy of Dario's base body
and shirt: **11.8 s** for the cut against **63.6 s** for the full drape of the same inputs (41.0 s of it Blender),
and the two `islands.json` are byte-identical (sha256 `387cd15f…`, also the sha of the three drapes the live world
had made of that shirt; `scripts/smoke_template_cut_islands_equal.py` repeats the proof on any body).

Key — the cut signature `cut_sig` (md5, 12 hex) over exactly what the cut reads:

| input | why |
|---|---|
| the params as `tsig` has them (`keyed_params`: without `TEXTURE_ONLY` / `MATERIAL_ONLY` and the other kinds' fields) minus `DRAPE_ONLY` = (`fabric`,) | every other field shapes `panel_spec`, `slit` or `uv_mode`; `fabric` reaches the builder only as the cloth settings, read after the cut |
| the base model sha256 | the body the panels are wrapped round, laid out of and measured on |
| the drape key's lower-layer tokens (`under_tokens`) plus one `collider:<item>` per lower template drape that is READY | the drapes / fits under the piece are the colliders `clear_of_body` lays the panels out of (only ready drapes are handed over); the jacket allowance and the chest shaping change the pattern or the body |
| `TEMPLATE_VERSION`, the flattening key (`uv_pattern_key`: a top's `TOP_UV_PATTERN_SHA`, a fitted piece's `UV_PATTERN_SHA` = md5 of `uv_pattern.py`) | a builder version or a new flattening re-cuts (seconds) |

No sig — and no Blender run — while a lower template layer is still to be draped (state `missing`): the next Build
drapes it first and lays this piece out of it, so a cut made now would differ from that drape; the dialog says
"Waiting for the layers under it to be draped" (`WAITING_LOWER`). A failed or blocked lower layer is no collider of
the drape either, so the cut goes on without it. Every kind outside `PATTERN_KINDS` gets its plain reason
(`NO_PATTERN`) and is never run; a fitted piece needs no bodice (its cut is `build_fitted`'s, section 1a), a skirt and a
strapless top are cut by `wrap` (section 1b).
`garment_template_cut.sig_of` is the one rule for the cut and for the drape's record.

Not in it: the views and the item picture (the drape key's texture part), `opacity`, `texture`, `sleeve_print`,
`fly`, the drape's frames / quality / poses / shape groups.

Files under `templates/cut/`: `{item}_{cut_sig}.islands.json` (the builder's bytes), `.cut.json` (the record: state,
metrics, `wall_s`) and `.pattern.png` (the debug sheet). The cut starts when the parameters are saved
(`PUT …/figure/template/{item_id}`) and whenever the dialog reads its Texture tab (`GET …/cut`), when it is missing —
never twice for one key, never again for a failed key, and not within 5 minutes after a run that judged nothing
(timeout, no Blender). A drape records the `cut_sig` it was laid out of when its colliders were the cut's
(own-mesh colliders shrunk or dropped by `drape_with_fallback` lay the panels out of other colliders: other islands).
A ready drape whose recorded `cut_sig` differs from the current one (a new `uv_pattern.py`, a lower layer that became
ready after it) counts as not draped (`garment_template.cut_behind`; `drape_state` "missing" with "The pattern was cut
anew — the next Build drapes it again") and the next Build drapes it again under the same `tsig`. The drape key is NOT
changed for this: putting the cut sig into `tsig` would re-drape every top once; this way only the tops whose cut
really moved re-drape, and a drape without a recorded `cut_sig` (made before cut-first, or a fallback drape) never
does.

## 1a. Step 3a: the close-fitting kinds

The fitted kinds (`FITTED_KINDS`: briefs, bra, gloves, tights, trousers — boxers are briefs with a leg) are cut from
the body's surface in `garment_template_fitted.build_fitted`; their pattern pieces come from that cut
(`app/blender/scripts/fitted_pattern.py`), the flattening is `uv_pattern.pattern_uv` as for a top. The planar box UV
these kinds had before is gone (its anisotropy on a cylinder: 27.6 against the pattern UV's 1.0, smoke
`smoke_uv_pattern.py` [18]).

**Seam planes first, labels after.** `fitted_pattern.seam_planes` gives each kind's seam planes (point, normal,
optionally one side or one arm region); `build_fitted` bisects the mesh along them (`bmesh.ops.bisect_plane`,
`clear_outer=False`) and only then labels every face by its centroid (`fitted_pattern.parts`), so every seam is a
vertex line and no face straddles it. The label is the int face layer `uv_part`, the cut lines the int edge layer
`uv_seam`; both ride through the evening and laying out into `garment_template_build.pattern_uv_of_fitted`, which
writes `UVMap` and `islands.json` and removes the layers.

| kind | pieces | seam planes / rule |
|---|---|---|
| trousers, boxers | `front_l/r`, `back_l/r`, `waistband`, `cuff_l/r` (hem cuff) | centre front / back (x = cx), the side seam per leg (through hip and ankle, normal turned to −Y), waistband under `top_y`; the waistband opens at the centre back (`fly` is texture only) |
| tights | `leg_l/r`, `gusset`, `waistband`, `top_band_l/r` (stay-ups) | centre plane, the gusset box (`TIGHTS_GUSSET_HALF_M`, `TIGHTS_GUSSET_DROP_M`); each leg opened along its inseam, a closed foot also along the sole (`SOLE_BELOW_ANKLE_M`) |
| briefs | `front`, `back`, `gusset`, `waistband`, `hip_band_l/r` (`sides` string) | front / back at cy; the gusset spans the whole crotch between `GUSSET_FRONT_M` before and `GUSSET_BACK_M` behind the crotch point, under `GUSSET_RISE_M` over it (no x bound: its sides are the leg openings) |
| bra | `cup_l/r`, `cup_top_l/r`, `gore`, `band_l/r`, `strap_l/r` | cup > gore > strap > band by `bra_region`'s own predicates, taken again after the bisect; a split face whose centroid left every region keeps the class it had before (face layer `fp_pre`) |
| gloves | `glove_back_l/r`, `palm_l/r`, `thumb_l/r`, `cuff_l/r` | per region (upper arm, forearm, hand) a dorsal / palmar plane through the bone line, moved to the middle of that region's cloth (`glove_centres`: the bone lies off the middle of the limb); the thumb past Thumb2 is a piece of its own, opened along its line facing the index finger |

**Every island a disk.** A tube (a leg, a finger, an arm) is opened by `fitted_pattern.cuts` (`plane_cut`: the edges
on a cut plane, on one half of it); whatever is still no disk (Euler characteristic < 1) is opened by
`open_islands` along the shortest edge path between its boundary loops. Islands under `MIN_ISLAND_FACES` faces are
reported (`slivers`), never relabelled.

**Rings are strips.** The waistband, the bra band halves, the hip bands, the cuffs and the stay-up bands are not
flattened but unrolled (`uv_pattern.ring_strip_uv`): u = the chord sum round the ring (the ring's own radius at each
angle, averaged over `RING_SMOOTH_RAD`), v = the height; theta = 0 is the ring's cut (the vertices the island holds
twice). The ring axis comes from the labelled geometry (`fitted_pattern.ring_centres`). Their stretch is recorded
(`strip_stretch_p95`), not gated.

**Rows.** `uv_pattern.ROWS[kind]` lays the islands out per kind (e.g. trousers: the four panels in one row, the bands
under them), so one px/cm holds for the whole atlas as for a top.

**Solver.** The fitted pieces are large (a tights leg ≈ 22 k vertices). `pattern_uv` with a `tol` (the fitted kinds:
`FITTED_ARAP_TOL` = 1e-3) stops ARAP when its energy changes by less than that, and solves LSCM and every ARAP step
directly (`uv_pattern._Banded`: reverse Cuthill-McKee order, block Cholesky; factor entries under `TINY` flushed —
subnormal fill-in made LAPACK 100× slower); a bandwidth over `BAND_MAX` or a matrix that does not factor falls back to
Jacobi-preconditioned CG. A top keeps the plain CG and its payload byte for byte (smoke [21]). Measured: Kira's
tights 520 s → 22 s in Blender, Lirien's / Kai's trousers 51–53 s → 7–8 s.

**Briefs (D8, D9).** `leg_cut` reaches 1.5: past 1 the leg line meets the side's height `reach` from the centre
instead of at the hip's side (`garment_template.brief_leg_shape`: reach = hip_half − (leg_cut − 1) / 0.5 × `HIGH_LEG`
(0.6) × (hip_half − g), the power held at 0.4 — Glut: 16.87 cm at 1.0, 8.55 cm at 1.5). `gusset_m` (0.03–0.08,
default 0.06) is the crotch's full width (`fitted_spec`'s `gusset_half_m` = half of it); the back's half width at the
crotch is `brief_back_width` = 0.4 g + back_coverage × 0.9 × (hip_half − 0.4 g). At the default it leaves the drape
key as it was, and it keys no other kind. The dialog shows it as "Crotch width".

**Texture from cut (group A, 2026-11-21).** Every fitted part has a prompt name (`PROMPT_PIECE_NAMES`, left / right
are the wearer's) and a sheet label (`PART_LABELS`); `piece_name_of` names the piece by its cut (high-leg briefs,
thong, elbow-length gloves, knee-high socks, capri trousers, …). `KIND_FIELDS` of every fitted kind carries `texture`
and `sleeve_print`, so the template dialog offers Texture = "From cut" through its generic field rendering, and the
Build's step "cut_texture" finishes such a piece like a top (section 2):

| kind | MAIN pieces (model answer, gates, fabric patch) | small pieces (fabric patch) | jobs |
|---|---|---|---|
| trousers, boxers | front: `front_r` + `front_l`; back: `back_l` + `back_r` | `waistband`, `cuff_l/r` | front, back (0 when the back view is plain) |
| briefs | front: `front`; back: `back` | `gusset`, `waistband`, `hip_band_l/r` | front, back (0 when plain) |
| tights | front: `leg_r` + `leg_l` | `gusset`, `waistband`, `top_band_l/r` | front only |
| bra | front: `cup_r` + `cup_l` (a balconette's `cup_top_l/r` also from the answer) | `gore`, `band_l/r`, `strap_l/r` | front only (a bandeau: front / back) |
| gloves | front: `glove_back_r` + `glove_back_l`; back: `palm_l` + `palm_r` | `thumb_l/r`, `cuff_l/r` | front, back (0 when plain) |

`garment_cut_texture.MAIN_PARTS` (per job; part names are unique across kinds, so a pattern finds its own) is what the
colour / detail gates measure, what the fabric patch (`panel_fabric`) is cut from and what a decorated prompt names.
The small pieces (`TRIM_PARTS`, together with a top's sleeves and cuffs) are filled deterministically with that patch,
like a top's sleeves; the piece's `sleeve_print` "picture" ("Small pieces: From the picture" in the Texture tab, the
dialog state's `trim` = `"pieces"`) takes them from the answer for an item whose picture shows a trim on them. The
fitted kinds have their OWN prompt files (`FITTED_PROMPT_FILES`: `garment_cut_texture_fitted_front_plain.md`,
`…_fitted_front_decorated.md`, `…_fitted_back.md`, four dialect sections each): the top's tested wording, the main
pieces named by where they lie (`main_words`: "the two largest shapes are the right front leg panel and the left
front leg panel", "the top left shape is the front panel"), the article of a pair noun ("a pair of gloves",
`a_piece`), the small pieces named as plain. Their sha (`prompt_sha_of(kind)`, `PROMPT_SHAS["fitted"]`) keys the
fitted atlases only; a top's `PROMPT_SHA` and atlases stay as they were. Before the cut, the Build's estimate counts a
back view and a back job only for a kind with back pieces (`kind_has_back`: not tights, not a cupped bra).

**Keys.** `tsig` of a fitted kind carries `FITTED_VERSION` = **1** (group A, 2026-11-21: released together with the
texture choice). **One-time re-drape:** every existing briefs, bra, gloves, tights and trousers drape made before it
(key without `fitted`, planar box UV) is due again on the next Build of its character — CPU only, 0 hub jobs, about a
minute per piece — and then carries its pattern islands; its texture stays the projection until the user chooses
"From cut". (It was held at 0 while nothing could use the islands.) `cut_sig` carries it and `FITTED_PATTERN_SHA` (md5 of
`fitted_pattern.py`), so a change of the cut rules re-cuts. A fitted piece's cut also keys `UV_PATTERN_SHA` (md5 of
`uv_pattern.py`); a top's keys `TOP_UV_PATTERN_SHA` instead — the md5 of the `uv_pattern.py` that last changed a top's
islands (c926b67f0). Step 3a's additions leave the tops' islands byte-identical, so no top drape counts as
`cut_behind` for them; a later change that alters a top's islands fails `scripts/smoke_uv_pattern.py` [21] and moves
the pinned sha and that smoke's base commit together.

## 1b. Step 3b: skirts and strapless tops (plan group B, phase A)

A skirt and a strapless top (`shoulders` strapless: bodice, bustier, corset) are built by `wrap` — two pygarment panels
(front, back; each 41 columns over half the circumference, a column = pi / 40) wrapped round the body axis, their side
seams merged by `remove_doubles`. `uv_pattern.wrap_parts` labels every face BEFORE that merge (the bmesh int layers
`uv_part` / `uv_seam` / `uv_cid` ride through it and through `split_slit`):

- skirt: front panel `skirt_front` (37), back panel `skirt_back` (38); the waistband (14) is the top
  `max(1, round(WAISTBAND_M[waistband] / row step))` face rows of both panels (thin 1.2 cm -> 1 row of 1.5 cm, wide
  3 cm -> 2 rows), opened at the back centre (the back panel's column 20 = pi / 2). The band's vertex rows hug the
  body like the pinned top row (`TOP_CLEAR_M`, 6 mm): a band standing off by `ROW_CLEAR_M` under a top row at 6 mm was
  a cone its strip could not unroll. Its UV is a construction strip over all its rows (`rows_strip_uv`: u along the
  mid line, v the slant up each column); on a low (hip) waist the band is still a cone — stretch p95 1.2–1.5 there,
  recorded, not gated (the band is filled with the panels' fabric unless `sleeve_print` is "picture").
- strapless top: front `front` (1), back `back` (2); a sweetheart dip stays in the front panel.
- a strapless dress (staged for phase D): the bodice above the row nearest `outline.waist_y` front / back, the skirt
  part under it `skirt_front` / `skirt_back`.

The panels are flattened round the vertical line through the body centre (+Z up). Layout: `pack` tries the kind's rows
(`ROWS["skirt"]` = the two panels side by side, the band under them; `ROWS["strapless"]` = front, back) AND
`ROWS_ALT["skirt"]` (the panels stacked) and keeps the larger px/cm — a wide skirt fills the square stacked.
New slit sides `front_left` / `front_right` (`SLIT_SIDES`): 6 columns (0.471 rad) beside the front centre
(`SLIT_ANGLE`) — a slit skirt, a wrap skirt's overlap edge; `left` / `right` lie on the side seams.

**Keys.** `PANEL_VERSION` = **1** is in `tsig` AND `cut_sig` of a panel piece (`panel_piece`: `PANEL_KINDS` =
(`skirt`,) and the strapless top), so every existing skirt and strapless top is re-draped ONCE on the next Build (CPU,
0 hub jobs); no other top and no fitted kind moves. Their cut keys the live `UV_PATTERN_SHA`. A skirt's new fields:
`waistband` (a new skirt defaults to `wide`; a saved skirt keeps the `thin` it was filled with) and `texture`.

Measured on copies of the world's bodies (cut only, 0 hub jobs): Kira's slit skirt 24.56 px/cm, Rosi's pencil skirt
20.25, Gelina's circle skirt 12.02 (stacked), Ylva's wrap skirt 13.30, Tilda's wool skirt 13.88, Rosi's corset top
16.82; panels area 0.97–1.00, stretch p95 1.03–1.28.

**Texture from cut (live 2026-11-22).** A skirt and a strapless top run the fitted prompt family under their prompt kind
(`garment_cut_texture.prompt_kind`: "skirt" / "strapless" — every csig of the piece, and `cut_info`'s look-up, is keyed
by it). Their plain trims (the waistband) are HELD (`HELD_TRIM_KINDS`): the template, the prompt's words and the gates
hold the model's parts only, the front job is zoomed like the back, and the trims are filled with the panel's fabric in
the atlas frame afterwards — a 21 px waistband strip beside the panel was thickened by MiniMax and dropped by P-H3.
Live on copies (chain MiniMax-H3-Image, P-H3-Image; 11 image jobs): Tilda's wool skirt, Rosi's corset and pencil skirt
(one Build), Gelina's circle skirt (stacked layout) — iou 0.997–0.999, colour dE 0.6–3.2, every back plain (fabric).

## 2. Texture from cut (step 2)

### The param `texture`

The template param `texture` chooses where a pattern piece's texture comes from (a top; since group A every fitted
kind, section 1a; since group B a skirt, section 1b): `"projection"` (default — the projection bake
of the piece-only views, always made and always inside the drape GLB) or `"cut"` (the cut atlas, swapped in at
assembly). It is in `TEXTURE_ONLY`: changing it costs no drape and no job, only a CPU re-assemble
(`garment_assembly` keeps `texture` = `"projection"` / `"cut:<csig>"` in its assembly state, `_apply_cut_atlas` puts
the atlas into the grafted mesh). With `texture` = `"cut"` the piece-only views are only the projection fallback: the
Build does not count them as due and a sheer piece is not blocked without them
(`garment_template_views.projection_optional`).

### The job — one click, two image jobs

One click in the template dialog = one flow (`garment_cut_texture.start` → `generate` in a thread; the Build runs
the same flow as its step "cut_texture"). Each texture job renders up to `CUT_ATTEMPTS` = 2 times: a GATE rejection
of attempt 1 renders attempt 2 on the routing chain's SECOND entry (`routing.gate_attempts`, route meta
`attempt_entry`, never `fallback_from`; a single-entry chain renders it on the same backend with the seed +
`ATTEMPT_SEED_STEP` 1000). A failure (busy, media off, no route, a dead backend after `run_routed`'s own re-runs) is
never retried; a failed attempt 2 keeps attempt 1's rejected answer. Every attempt's answer stays as
`.raw_{job}.a{n}.png`, the job's record entry lists them (`attempts`: backend, dialect, routing, state, reason,
metrics) and "Use this answer" can take either (`recompose(accept=job, attempt=n)`, route body `attempt`). Starting it sets the param `texture` to `"cut"` (`select_cut` — the result has no
other use) and the flow ends by applying itself (`apply`: `figure.invalidate` + `garment_assembly.schedule`, CPU) —
no second click (user finding 2026-11-20).

The cut is made by TWO routed image jobs (`JOBS` = front, back; `split_islands`):

| job | islands | Picture 2 | prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| front | every island except the back panel: front panel, sleeves, neckband, collar, cuffs | the item picture | `shared/templates/image/garment_cut_texture_front_plain.md`, `shared/templates/image/garment_cut_texture_front_decorated.md` |
| back | the back panel (`BACK_PARTS`: `back`; step 3a also a trouser's `back_l` / `back_r` and a glove's `palm_l` / `palm_r` — tights and a bra have no back job) | the piece's back view (`garment_template_views`, occasion `garment_view`) | `shared/templates/image/garment_cut_texture_back.md` |

**Sleeve rule:** sleeves (and band, collar, placket, cuffs) go with the front job — the item picture shows the front,
and a sleeve seen from the front carries its fabric there; only the back panel needs the back view. A pattern without a
back panel runs the front job only. Each job keeps the atlas frame (same size, same px/cm) with only its islands drawn.

1. The back view is made first when it is missing (`_ensure_back_view` → `garment_template_views.generate`, 1 job, at
   most 2); a refused view stops the flow with a plain reason before any cut job. The button counts it
   (`job_plan`: `back_view` + `upscale` + front + back = `total`, `max` with the view's second attempt).
2. The FULL-RES item picture, flattened on white. Below **`MIN_ITEM_SIDE` = 1024 px** (long side) it first goes
   through the routed `upscale` occasion (one extra job; Lanczos when that fails, and Lanczos tops up a result still
   below the minimum) — tiny item pictures gave a wrong logo.
3. Per job the CLEAN model template of its islands (`render_template`: the islands PRE-FILLED with the item's base
   fabric colour — `cut_texture_post.fabric_colour`, see below —, a thin outline inside the mask, white background,
   square, no text) and ONE routed img2img render (template first, reference second) on the
   occasion `garment_cut_texture`: `routing.run_routed` → `run_on_backend` → `run_on_backend_channel`. The BACK
   job's template is ZOOMED (`job_frame`: the back island's box + `BACK_ZOOM_MARGIN` 12 % per side, a white square
   scaled to the canvas, so the panel fills ~40 % instead of ~15 %); its answer is gated in that frame and put back
   into the atlas frame (`unzoom`) before the compose. On the un-zoomed template P-H3 filled the empty canvas with the
   back-view photo or an extra garment. The prompts are three shipped SECTIONED edit-prompt templates
   (`PROMPT_FILES`; one section per dialect of `app/imagegen/edit_prompt.py`, `docs/edit-prompts.md` §6): the render
   picks the section of the backend it lands on (`edit_prompt.dialect_of`, inside the routed render, so attempt 2 on
   another backend gets its own wording) and records it (`dialect`). MiniMax-H3-Image (`picture_subject`) gets its own
   subject-format paragraph (candidate N of the MiniMax A/B 2026-11-21, with the piece list, `layout_words` and the
   plain sleeves); P-H3 (`picture_edit`) and every other backend the SHORT prompts of the prompt test 2026-11-21
   (23 local P-H3 jobs on three real items; the long prompt with layout sentences invented emblems and prints): the
   front says "texture the sewing pattern with the fabric of the <piece>, keep every shape, its colour and the white
   background, add only the fine weave"; for a DECORATED item (`is_decorated`: the item picture's detail density >
   `DECORATED_DETAIL` 0.10 — code decides, never the model) it names the front island by its place (`front_place`,
   e.g. "top left") and asks for the same print or embroidery at the same place, the other shapes plain; the back
   prompt is the plain one on the back's fabric. Not world-overridable, `/admin/templates` stays LLM-only. The use
   case `garment_cut_texture` carries no style, negative or instruction — the prompt is the whole subject.
4. Post-processing per job (`app/core/cut_texture_post.py`, pure numpy/PIL): resample the answer to the template
   frame and register it raster-to-raster, gate it against the job's islands AND by the answer gates (below), cut it
   with bleed padding, colour-match it to the ITEM fabric (both jobs — one fabric, one colour), then fill the islands
   the model does not paint from the job's main panel (`fill_fabric`, below). A rejected or failed job stops the flow:
   the next job that needs a render is marked `skipped` and not spent, while a re-used passed answer (0 image jobs)
   keeps its `ready` entry, so "Use this answer" on the rejected one composes the atlas without rendering it again;
   the reason is prefixed `Front:` / `Back:`.
5. `compose`: the front job's grown islands, then the back job's grown islands outside the front islands, then each
   job's own islands — one atlas. Seam continuity is measured inside the jobs (`seam_de`) and across them
   (`cross_seams` → `seam_cross_de`, the front/back side seams); both are metrics, not gates.
6. The record + files go to `templates/cut/` (`{item}_{csig}.json`, `.template_front.png`, `.template_back.png`,
   `.item.png`, `.back_ref.png`, `.raw_front.png`, `.raw_back.png`, and `.atlas.png` only for state `ready`). The
   record keeps both answers under `jobs.front` / `jobs.back` (state, reason, metrics, backend, routing). States:
   `ready`, `rejected` (the gate's first failing measure is the reason), `failed` (the error, e.g. media off, no
   route, backend busy). "Redo front job" / "Redo back job" (`only`) re-run one job and re-use the other job's
   answer of the same csig (1 image job).

The job never holds `keyed_lock("character_profile", …)` and never submits from inside a GPU worker. Media off
(`MediaGenerationDisabled`), an empty chain (`NoRouteError`) and a piece without a cut (or without a picture) are
refused synchronously, before any thread or file exists. The drape is never asked: the flow may run while the Build
drapes the piece.

### Pre-fill, answer gates and the sleeve fabric (user test 2026-11-21)

Kai's plain blue T-shirt came back as an invented magenta shirt with a big text print, and without the words "graphic
print design" in the prompt as a red one; Dario's chest embroidery landed on one sleeve. Prompt wording alone does not
hold, so:

- **Pre-fill** — `cut_texture_post.fabric_colour(rgb, mask)`: the item picture box-filtered to 512 px, the garment
  mask (BiRefNet cut) eroded by 2 % of its extent, every pixel within r (1.5 % of the extent) of a DETAIL (|L* − local
  mean| > 6) dropped, then the peak of the a*b* histogram and the median L*a*b* within 12 of it. Kai → (30, 90, 173),
  Dario → (19, 50, 29). A fill nearer to white than 40 is darkened to it (`PREFILL_MIN_DIST`).
- **Answer gates** (`ANSWER_GATE`, measured on the job's main panel — front / back — of the registered answer):
  colour = CIEDE2000 of its `fabric_colour` against the item's, limit **20** (Kai 35.0 / 43.5 rejected, Dario 9.9
  passes; an item picture with no plain fabric — `plain` < `FABRIC_MIN_PLAIN` 0.05, an all-over
  pattern such as Glut's scales, whose histogram peak is the dark gaps — gets the colour gate `skipped`, live
  2026-11-21: the faithful red MiniMax answers were rejected at ΔE00 26.9 / 30.6); invented content = `detail_density` (share of L* edges > 4 per px at 128 px) at most **4 × Picture 2's +
  0.04** (Kai 0.47 > 0.10 rejected, Dario 0.21 < 0.43 passes). The record keeps `metrics.gates`, `fabric`, `detail`
  and `failures` per job; the dialog shows each check.
- **Sleeves and neckband** are not taken from the model: `fill_fabric` cuts the plainest `PATCH_CM` = 8 cm window of
  the main panel (within ΔE76 10 of its base colour), tiles it mirrored (seamless) and moves its Lab mean onto the
  panel's base colour, over every grown pixel whose nearest island is a sleeve, cuff or the neckband. The template
  param **`sleeve_print`** (`"plain"` default / `"picture"`, `TEXTURE_ONLY`, set in the Texture tab via
  `POST …/cut/sleeves`) takes the model's sleeves and cuffs for a garment with a print on a sleeve; a switch composes
  the atlas again on the CPU (`recompose`); until then `cut_info` says `"option"`. The back job paints the back panel
  alone anyway.
- **Use this answer** (`POST …/cut/use {job}`, `start_recompose` → `recompose`, 0 image jobs): the shown answer of a
  job — also a rejected one, after an in-app confirmation naming the reason — is accepted (`accepted`,
  `accepted_over`) and composed; a job without an answer stays `skipped` with "Not made yet", and Run all renders just
  that one. Redo front / back stay as they were.

- **A plain back gets no job** (live E2E 2026-11-21: P-H3 invented a collar and a button placket on Dario's plain
  back, fold shading and a colour fringe on Tilda's). The back view's detail density (the same
  `picture_reference` measure as `DECORATED_DETAIL`, `back_detail`, cached by the file's stat) at or below
  `PLAIN_BACK_DETAIL` **0.20** makes the back plain (`is_plain_back`): no back hub job, the back islands and their
  bleed are tiled with the FRONT job's panel fabric — the same patch and base colour as the sleeves
  (`panel_fabric` → `fill_plain_back`), so the side seams meet one colour. Derived like the front threshold from the
  measured pictures: plain shirt backs Kai 0.018, Tilda 0.054, Dario 0.158 (his drape folds are every edge pixel), the
  knit cardigan 0.246; a painted-in back print 0.07–0.23 (a logo on 20 % of the garment) and 0.26–0.36 (a text print
  on 35 %). 0.20 = 1.3 × Dario, the front's margin (0.10 = 1.3 × Tilda's 0.076). It gives up a small print on a dark
  folded back (filled plain). The record's `back_plain` and `jobs.back.source` `"fabric"` say it; the plan, the
  Build's cost note and the figure row count 0 back jobs; Redo back is not offered. A DECORATED back keeps its job,
  its detail gate from the back view's own detail (`references` → `detail.back`). A missing back view is made first
  and the counts assume the back job until it is measured.

`POST_VERSION` 10 and the reworded prompts (`PROMPT_SHA`) make every older cut atlas stale.

### One hub-job flow per piece

`app/core/piece_jobs.py`: the cut flow (`"cut"`) and each piece-only view (`"view:front"` / `"view:back"`) claim the
piece `(character, item)` before they start; a second claim is refused with the holder's plain reason (`Busy`, the
routes answer 409). The dialog mirrors it: while one flow runs every job button of the piece is off and says why
(`blocked_reason` of the cut payload, `busy` of the views payload). The Dario test spent two needless view jobs while
the cut ran, and the new views changed the drape key so the shirt dropped off the figure.

### A cut-textured piece keeps its drape

With `texture` = `"cut"` the piece-only views only feed the projection fallback; a changed view changes `tsig` (the
projection is baked into the drape) but nothing the figure shows. `garment_template.cut_stand_in` therefore lets the
newest accepted drape whose `tsig` differs ONLY in its texture part (recomputed exactly from the record's
`texture_sha`) stand in as `ready` (`drape_state` … `stand_in: true`) — no re-drape, no drop-off. A record without
`texture_sha` (older drapes) never stands in; with `"projection"` the drape is due as before.

### The occasion `garment_cut_texture`

Admin label **"Garment — texture from cut"** (`app/imagegen/occasions.py`), world-scoped, a no-fold occasion
(`NO_FOLD_OCCASIONS`: nothing moves to a negative prompt). It hands over two pictures, so `min_ref_slots` 2 — a
1-slot backend never fits. `requires_chain` is set: an empty chain is `NoRouteError` (pointing to /admin/settings →
Media Generation → Routing), never a cheapest — possibly paid — pick. The occasion catalog has 41 occasions with it.

Required backend entry for P-H3-Image (references as `<Picture 1>` / `<Picture 2>`, dialect `picture_edit`):
`ref_slot_count` ≥ 2, category `img2img`, and

```json

```

The upsampler rewrites the prompt; a P-H3 backend without that entry still runs, and the record marks
`upsampler_on: true` (`edit_prompt.lint_backend` code `upsampler_on`, also shown in the routing overview).

### The gate and the post-processing constants

`cut_texture_post.CUT_GATE` — a rejected answer leaves the projection on the figure:

| measure | meaning | limit |
|---|---|---|
| `coverage` | share of the island mask that is foreground (not near-white) | ≥ 0.97 |
| `island_coverage` | the worst single island's coverage | ≥ 0.90 |
| `iou` | foreground against the island mask | ≥ 0.90 |
| `spill` | foreground beyond `SPILL_CM` outside the mask, as a share of the mask area | ≤ 0.03 |

- `SPILL_CM` = 1.0 cm — foreground closer than that to an island is a smeared edge, not spill.
- `BLEED_CM` = 1.0 cm — every island is regrown that far past its border (the model's outline is eroded away first),
  so mip-maps and bilinear lookups at a seam never read the background.
- The erosion is the answer's own outline width (`outline_rim`): ring k = the pixels k px inside the mask; a ring
  whose share of pixels more than `RIM_DE` 12 (ΔE76) from the median cloth colour 10–20 px deep exceeds that band's
  own share by `RIM_SHARE` 0.05 still belongs to the outline; the run of such rings from the border plus one pixel,
  clamped to 3–20 px (`RIM_MIN_PX` / `RIM_MAX_PX`). The record keeps it under `metrics.rim`. Live 2026-11-21:
  MiniMax-H3 keeps a ~5 px outline and a registration a few pixels off pushes it deeper into one side, which the
  fixed 3 px left as a grey line along Tilda's side seams (6 px now); a P-H3 answer measures 5 px. A second, FINE
  pass does the same at `RIM_DE_FINE` 5 against the band 20–30 px deep (`RIM_REF_FINE_PX`, `metrics.rim.fine`) and
  the longer run wins: MiniMax shaded the border of Lirien's plain leather with a soft darker band (L* 21–26 at 3–10
  px against 27.6 inside, ΔE 5–8, live 2026-11-22) that the coarse pass stopped at 7 px; the bleed drew it out as
  dark rays along every seam. It measures 14 px now.
- `CLAMP_DE` = 25.0 — the Lab transfer onto the item's fabric (BiRefNet matte, trimmed luminance range) moves the
  colour by at most this ΔE76; the record keeps `de_before` / `de_after` / `by` / `gain` under `metrics.colour` (the
  test jobs were too saturated). When the item AND the answer have plain fabric (`plain` ≥ `FABRIC_MIN_PLAIN`) the
  shift is the answer's base colour onto the item's (`fabric_colour`, the two the colour gate compares, `by` "base");
  otherwise mean onto mean (`by` "mean": an all-over pattern). The contrast gain is capped at `SIGMA_GAIN_MAX` 1.0 —
  the match never adds contrast: a photo's sigma is shading and highlights. Live 2026-11-22, Lirien's glossy brown
  leather: the item's pixel mean L* 34.1 sat 5 above its base 29.1 and its sigma L* 8.6 against the plain answer's
  1.1 gave a 7.7 x gain — the faithful answer (ΔE00 1.0 from the item) came out pale taupe with a weave of noise.
- `POST_VERSION` = 10 — bump it whenever the post-processing changes an atlas; it is part of `csig` (2: the
  post-processing runs per job and the two answers are composed; 3: pre-fill, answer gates, sleeve fabric fill; 4:
  foreground pieces apart from the islands dropped before the registration; 5: the back job in its zoom frame; 6: a
  plain back is the front's fabric, no back job; 7: the erosion is the answer's outline width, `outline_rim`; 8: only a whole island thinner than the erosion keeps
  its own pixels, a thin part of a cored island — a fitted border's jagged teeth, a corner — is regrown from the core:
  the teeth kept the light outline as bleed seed, a pale zigzag along Kai's jeans waist seam, live 2026-11-21; 9: the
  composed atlas fills its background pockets, `fill_pockets`; 10: the colour match base onto base without added
  contrast, the fine rim pass).
- `POCKET_CM` = 3.0 cm — after the compose, every background pixel this close to the islands + bleed, and every
  pocket they enclose, takes its nearest filled colour (`fill_pockets`, exact distance transform): the round notch
  between the two front leg islands of Lirien's trousers stayed white past the 1 cm bleed and showed as a white spot
  at the crotch seam (live 2026-11-22).
- A SHEER piece (fabric `sheer`) whose cut atlas shows skin in the gaps of a lace (`lace_alpha`: 10–70 %
  of the fabric pixels skin-coloured, `LACE_SKIN_SHARE` / `LACE_SKIN_MAX`, the YCrCb box of `skin_mask`) is keyed
  into an RGBA atlas at assembly (`garment_assembly._lace_keyed`): the gaps become the sheer base cloth — the
  threads' median colour at the piece's `opacity` — the threads stay opaque, and the material's base colour alpha
  is 1 (the texture's alpha carries the opacity) and the piece has no sheen, clear coat or specular
  (`garment_assembly.LACE_SURFACE`). The stored atlas stays RGB; `LACE_VERSION` is part of the
  assembly's texture identity. The model paints skin into the gaps although the prompt says "without skin"
  (Kira's lace tights, live 2026-11-22: grey-beige pantyhose on the figure). Outside that range — a plain sheer
  cloth, or a cloth that is itself skin-coloured (brown leather is 99 % "skin" in that box) — the uniform opacity
  stays. Measured on Kira's tights (Blender `figure_front`, uniform white world; legs' 20th / 80th luminance
  percentile, torso skin (200, 162, 129)): with the cotton preset's sheen the threads came out (94, 92, 92) and the gaps
  a grey (134, 127, 126) — the grey veil and the light rim of the review; sheen off (51, 49, 49) / (116, 91, 83); sheen
  and specular off (35, 31, 32) — the atlas's thread exactly — / (113, 85, 76), 0.57 / 0.52 / 0.59 of the torso skin
  where the item picture's gaps sit at 0.57 / 0.56 / 0.59 of its bare thigh. Double-sided stays (single-sided:
  gaps (142, 106, 93), lighter than the item). `LACE_VERSION` 2.
- Seam continuity (`seam_continuity`, the median ΔE76 across the `seams` pairs) is a metric, not a gate.

### The key `csig`

`csig` = md5 of (`islands_sha` of the cut's `islands.json`, `item_sha` of the item picture, `back_sha` of the back view the back job reads ("" without a back panel), `PROMPT_SHA` of the three prompt files of the piece's family — a top's, or the fitted kinds' own (`prompt_sha_of(kind)`) —, `POST_VERSION`), 12 hex.

It deliberately does not contain the drape key `tsig`: `tsig` also carries the piece-only views, and a re-rendered
FRONT view changes nothing the cut atlas depends on. The back view does: a new back view makes the cut stale (the
Views tab says so next to it). A re-drape with new islands (new body, new params, a version bump), a
new item picture, a changed prompt or post version gives a new `csig`; the figure then shows the projection and the
row says "cut out of date" (`cut_info` state `stale`) — an old atlas is never painted onto new islands.

The atlas is made from the CUT's islands and mapped onto a drape by the drape's islands sha: every drape of the same
cut writes the same bytes, so the atlas stays valid across re-drapes (new views, a retry, a new fabric) as long as the
cut is unchanged. A drape of another cut never gets it: when a ready atlas exists for other islands, made after the
drape, the row says either that the drape is of an older cut and the next Build drapes the new one (`cut_info` state
`drape_behind` — only when the drape's recorded `cut_sig` differs from the current cut's, `cut_behind`), or that the
drape was laid out of other layers than the cut and the cut texture does not fit it (`drape_other`: a fallback drape
or one without a recorded `cut_sig`, which no Build re-drapes for the cut).

`garment_template.cut_info` is the one place that decides what a piece shows: `texture` `"cut"` only when the param is
`"cut"` and a `ready` record with its atlas exists for the current `csig`; otherwise `"projection"` with `cut_state`
`off` / `missing` / `stale` / `drape_behind` / `drape_other` / `rejected` / `failed` / `option`. `atlas_for` returns the atlas bytes for the assembly.
